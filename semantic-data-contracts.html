Does unchanged physical type mean unchanged business meaning?

No. A→B is structural allowed-value drift ([10000, 20000]), introduced by reviewer policy; the original receipt becomes STALE_CONTRACT and requires revalidation. B→C is a reviewed semantic owner-identity change and contract restructuring; the Loan Identifier remains STRING, but the comparator reports semantic identity and relationship drift. Requiredness in C is reviewer policy, not a source-selected constraint. A→T compares different sealed assets: the Principal Amount definition changes from excluding interest and other charges to including capitalized interest but excluding other charges, while INTEGER is unchanged. That is native DEFINITION_DRIFT, not same-asset definition drift.

The missing required identifier case has one affected concept, one affected source, one affected relationship and one violation in the native impact response. Nullability remains UNKNOWN. This is recorded native validation, not a live arbitrary browser call or authorization to act.

What a contract binds

A contract can bind field identity, definition, domain, category, established role/class, physical type and evidence-supported constraints to a revision. Strict validation does not silently coerce strings to numbers.

Illustrative fieldConstraintExample
amountnumber; explicit currency1250 accepted by example type check; "1250" rejected

Validation receipts identify the contract/revision. Physical, structural, definition, domain, relationship or semantic drift can make a downstream dependency stale; bounded impact analysis identifies affected bindings. A pass is not business permission.
