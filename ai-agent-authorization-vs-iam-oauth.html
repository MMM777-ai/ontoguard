AI agent authorization vs IAM and OAuth

IAM and OAuth govern identity and access; some implementations also bind transaction details.

How it works

The relevant question is whether existing controls cover the exact action, trusted current authority, pre-effect recheck, consume uniqueness, recovery and correlated proof. A customer already covering these controls may not need an additional runtime. No blanket claim that OAuth cannot inspect or bind actions.

Illustrative explanations describe the boundary, not a customer result. For specific evidence and integration scope see the linked product page.

Complement, not blanket replacement

IAM and OAuth can grant scoped access and some transaction-bound systems can enforce exact details. An additional action-authorization layer is useful only where the existing stack lacks current authoritative recheck, protected consume or correlated outcome evidence. Evaluate a changed target and expired authority through the actual protected route, rather than assuming a token is insufficient in every design.

Technical note by OntoGuard · product evidence and review scope are identified in the linked materials.

Evidence and buyer questions

How does DA differ from IAM?

IAM and OAuth can provide identity, access and transaction-binding controls. Decision Authorization adds an exact-action authorization and evidence lifecycle at a properly integrated execution seam, including revalidation of current trusted context. These controls can complement one another.
